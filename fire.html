Course Prerequisites

These are the prerequisites for SFFMA Firefighter I and II certification. Finish them before starting the hazmat class. Each listing tells you what the course covers and where to find it. All of the online courses are free, so save each completion certificate. To track your progress and upload certificates, use the checklist on your My Account page.

NIMS / ICS (FEMA Independent Study)

IS-100.c: Introduction to the Incident Command System

The basics of ICS: how command is set up, common terms, span of control, and how incidents are organized. Self-paced online with a short final exam.

Open course →

IS-200.c: Basic ICS for Initial Response

Builds on IS-100. Covers the first-arriving responder's role, briefings, delegating authority, and expanding or transferring command. Take IS-100 first.

Open course →

IS-700.b: Introduction to the National Incident Management System

The big picture of NIMS: how agencies at every level share resources, use common terms, and work together under one system.

Open course →

IS-800.d: National Response Framework, An Introduction

How the nation responds to disasters: roles of local, state, tribal, and federal partners, plus the Emergency Support Functions.

Open course →

FEMA courses need a free FEMA SID number before you can take the exams. You can get one at cdp.dhs.gov/FEMASID.

Roadway Safety

National Traffic Incident Management (TIM) Responder Training

FHWA course on working safely at roadway incidents: apparatus blocking, traffic control zones, scene clearance, and hi-vis safety. Web-based course FHWA-NHI-133126A, about 4 hours, with a final exam (70% to pass).

How to register →
Firefighter Safety and Health

Courage to Be Safe (NFFF)

National Fallen Firefighters Foundation program on changing the culture of firefighter safety, based on the 16 Life Safety Initiatives and Everyone Goes Home. Self-paced online through the Fire Hero Learning Network.

Open course →

Cancer Awareness (Firefighter Cancer Support Network)

Firefighter cancer risks and how to cut your exposure: gross decon, clean gear, SCBA use, screenings, and healthy habits. Texas (TCFP) uses the FCSN Cancer Awareness Training. Check with your instructor that this is the version they accept.
