CLASS 1 — Maximum Protection

Chart in words

Class 1: fully encapsulating vapor-protective ensemble with SCBA, for unknown or IDLH vapor hazards and skin-absorption hazards. Roughly comparable to OSHA/EPA Level A; the two systems are not identical.

CLASS 2 — Max Respiratory Protection

Class 2: worn with a CBRN SCBA for IDLH environments with high levels of chemical vapors, liquids, or particulates. It covers vapors as well as liquids.

CLASS 3 — Low Vapor + Splash Protection

Class 3: worn with a CBRN APR or PAPR for low levels of chemical vapors, liquids, or particulates where the atmosphere is not IDLH and the contaminant is known and monitored.

CLASS 4 — Bio + Radiological Particulate

Chart in words

Class 4: CBRN APR or PAPR with particulate-resistant clothing, for biological agents and radiological particulates only. No protection against chemical vapors, gases, liquid splashes, or oxygen deficiency.

SKIN PROTECTION — Clothing Categories

Chart in words

  • Thermal protective clothing (structural gear, proximity, fire entry, flame-resistant, cold weather): structural gear is not vapor-tight.
  • Chemical protective clothing: vapor-protective, liquid splash-protective encapsulating, and nonencapsulating. No suit resists every chemical.
  • Ballistic protection does not protect against chemicals.
CPC FAILURE — Modes

Chart in words

  • Permeation: the chemical passes through the material at the molecular level with no visible sign.
  • Degradation: the material breaks down (swelling, discoloration, stiffening).
  • Penetration: the chemical gets through openings such as zippers, seams, or tears.
PPE SELECTION — Decision Guide

Selecting PPE

  1. Identify the hazard: material, concentration, oxygen level, physical state, and routes of exposure.
  2. Match the task: entry, decon, or support work each have different exposures.
  3. Choose respiratory protection: SCBA for unknown, IDLH, or oxygen-deficient conditions; APR or PAPR only when the contaminant is known, measured below IDLH, oxygen is at least 19.5%, and the cartridge is approved for it.
  4. Choose clothing compatible with the chemical (check the manufacturer's chemical resistance data).
  5. Reassess as conditions change.

Decon Team PPE

Choose decon team PPE from a hazard and task assessment. Workers who first contact people and equipment leaving the hot zone may need the same protection as the entry team. A lower level is acceptable only when the assessment shows the exposure is lower, and that decision should be documented.

Course answer discrepancy: earlier study material said decon personnel always wear one level below the entry team. Guidance says decon workers may need the same level as the entry team, and only some may be adequately protected one level lower.
