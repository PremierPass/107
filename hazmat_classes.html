Class 1 — EXPLOSIVES
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Divisions

  • 1.1 mass explosion hazard
  • 1.2 projection hazard
  • 1.3 fire hazard with a minor blast or projection hazard
  • 1.4 minor explosion hazard
  • 1.5 very insensitive, with a mass explosion hazard
  • 1.6 extremely insensitive

The letter after the division (for example 1.1D) is a compatibility group for loading. It is not a separate division.

Placarding: 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3 are Table 1 materials (placard any quantity); 1.4, 1.5, and 1.6 are Table 2.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 2 — GASES
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Divisions

  • 2.1 Flammable gas (red placard). Example: LNG, UN1972, Guide 115.
  • 2.2 Nonflammable, nonpoisonous gas (green placard; oxygen has its own yellow placard). Example: nitrogen, refrigerated liquid, UN1977, Guide 120.
  • 2.3 Poison gas (white placard, Table 1: placard any quantity). Example: chlorine, UN1017, Guide 124, highlighted green in the ERG index.

"Inhalation Hazard" is a marking required on some packages; it is not a separate placard or division.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 3 — FLAMMABLE LIQUIDS
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Flammable liquid: flash point of 60°C (140°F) or lower. Examples: gasoline (UN1203), acetone, ethanol.

Different flammable liquids use different guides, for example nonpolar fuels versus polar or water-miscible liquids, which need alcohol-resistant foam. Look up the exact entry.

Distances for a Class 3 liquid come from its orange guide. Only materials highlighted green in the index have Table 1 protective action distances.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 4 — FLAMMABLE SOLIDS
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Divisions

  • 4.1 Flammable solid (red and white striped placard): includes self-reactive and desensitized explosive materials.
  • 4.2 Spontaneously combustible (white top, red bottom): may ignite on contact with air.
  • 4.3 Dangerous when wet (blue placard, Table 1: placard any quantity): gives off flammable or toxic gas with water. Some 4.3 materials are highlighted green; ERG Table 2 lists the gases they produce.

There is no Division 4.4.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 5 — OXIDIZERS & ORGANIC PEROXIDES
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Divisions

  • 5.1 Oxidizer (yellow placard): gives up oxygen and can make other materials burn more intensely. Example: ammonium nitrate.
  • 5.2 Organic peroxide (red top, yellow bottom): unstable; can decompose violently from heat, shock, or contamination. Type B, temperature controlled, is a Table 1 material.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 6 — TOXIC & INFECTIOUS
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Divisions

  • 6.1 Poisonous (toxic) material (white placard). Materials that are poisonous by inhalation use the POISON INHALATION HAZARD placard (Table 1: placard any quantity).
  • 6.2 Infectious substance: cultures, patient specimens, regulated medical waste.

Toxic gases such as carbon monoxide and chlorine are Division 2.3, not Class 6.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 7 — RADIOACTIVE
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Labels on packages are Radioactive White-I, Yellow-II, and Yellow-III, based on radiation level at the package surface and the transport index. Vehicles carrying Yellow-III labeled packages must display the RADIOACTIVE placard (Table 1).

The transport index (TI) is the radiation level at 1 meter from the package.

Protect yourself with time, distance, and shielding, and use detection equipment. Radiation can't be seen, smelled, or felt.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

Class 8 — CORROSIVES
Chart removed: the earlier poster for this topic contained errors, so its key information is shown here as text.

Corrosive: causes full-thickness destruction of skin or corrodes steel or aluminum. Examples: sulfuric acid, sodium hydroxide.

Corrosives use several different guides depending on the material (for example, whether it is also toxic, combustible, or water-reactive). Some corrosives are highlighted green.

ERG guide: no single guide covers a whole hazard class. Find the 4-digit UN/NA number (placard, orange panel, label, or shipping papers) in the yellow pages, or the proper shipping name in the blue pages, and use the guide listed for that exact entry.

NFPA 704 — Full Diamond System

Chart in words

  • NFPA 704 is for fixed facilities, not transportation.
  • Blue (left): health. Red (top): flammability. Yellow (right): instability. Each is rated 0 (minimal) to 4 (severe).
  • White (bottom): special hazards. NFPA 704 recognizes only W (water reactive), OX (oxidizer), and SA (simple asphyxiant).
BLUE — Health Hazard

Chart in words

Health hazard, 0 to 4. 4: short exposure could cause death or major residual injury. 0: no significant health hazard beyond ordinary combustibles.

RED — Flammability Hazard

Chart in words

Flammability, 0 to 4. 4: burns readily at normal temperatures (very low flash point gases and liquids). 0: will not burn.

YELLOW — Instability / Reactivity

Chart in words

Instability, 0 to 4. 4: readily capable of detonation at normal temperatures and pressures. 0: normally stable, even in fire.

WHITE — Special Hazards

Chart in words

Special hazards. NFPA 704 recognizes only W (reacts with water), OX (oxidizer), and SA (simple asphyxiant). Other letters or symbols (COR, CRYO, radiation, biohazard) are sometimes used locally but are not part of the standard.

Notes on Removed Class Charts

Why the class posters were removed: each one assigned ERG guide numbers to whole classes or divisions that don't match the ERG (for example, Class 9 listed Guide 171 for lithium batteries and dry ice, which use 147 and 120). Several also showed placards or divisions that don't exist (such as a "Division 4.4," a separate "Inhalation Hazard" placard, or carbon monoxide as Class 6) and invented isolation distance tables. The text versions on this page replace them.
NFPA 704 white section: the standard recognizes only W (water reactive), OX (oxidizer), and SA (simple asphyxiant). Other letters or symbols sometimes seen there (COR, CRYO, radiation, biohazard) are not part of NFPA 704.
